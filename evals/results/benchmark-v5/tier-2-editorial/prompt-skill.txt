# Jol · Issue 07: "The Last Boatbuilders of Sadarghat"
Tier: 2 — Intermediate
Why this tier: a long read earns section entrances and scroll choreography that pace the story and mark its turns, but it is read, not watched, so pinned film sequences or real-time 3D (Tiers 4–5) would fight the text, and a static page (Tier 1) would waste the issue's rhythm.

## The client
*Jol* (জল, "water") is an independent online magazine about Bangladesh's rivers, published in quarterly issues of one long feature each. Issue 07 is a 4,000-word reported piece by Farhana Rahman on the wooden-boat builders along the Buriganga at Sadarghat, with photographs by Tanvir Hossain. Readers arrive from newsletters and social posts, most on phones.

## What they need
- Carry a reader through one long article from first line to author note without losing them.
- Give the piece's six to eight movements a felt rhythm: each section arrives, pull quotes land, images take their space.
- Keep the text the hero: comfortable to read for twenty minutes on a 375 px phone.
- Credit the writer and photographer properly and point to the next issue.

## Pages
- One page: the article (issue cover opening, the feature, footnotes, author note, issue footer).

## Content
- Masthead: *Jol*, Issue 07, Monsoon 2026.
- Headline: "The Last Boatbuilders of Sadarghat". Standfirst: "On the Buriganga's south bank, a dozen families still build wooden cargo boats by eye. The river that made them is the thing ending them."
- Byline: Farhana Rahman. Photographs: Tanvir Hossain. Reading time: 18 minutes.
- Seven sections, each with a heading and 450–650 words (the agent may write the body text from the facts below; no invented statistics, quotes are only the ones given):
  1. "Keel" — the yard at dawn; a keel of sal wood laid on the mud.
  2. "By Eye" — no drawings; master builder Abdul Karim (62) sets each rib by sight.
  3. "Teak and Tar" — timber now comes from Myanmar; prices doubled since 2019 (the client's figure).
  4. "The Steel Yards" — steel launches being welded across the river, faster and cheaper.
  5. "Monsoon" — work stops when the river rises; families move up the bank.
  6. "Apprentices" — two apprentices left this year; one, Rubel (19), stayed.
  7. "Launch" — a finished boat is pushed into the river by forty men.
- Pull quotes (use exactly): "I don't draw a boat. I see it, then I build what I saw." — Abdul Karim. "The river used to need us. Now it only needs steel." — a builder who asked not to be named.
- Inline images: six photographs, supplied later as files; the site holds their places with aspect ratios (3:2 ×4, 4:5 ×2) and captions (the agent writes captions from the section facts).
- Footnotes: five, linked both ways (marker → note → back), e.g. on sal wood, the 2019 price figure, the Sadarghat terminal.
- Author note: "Farhana Rahman reports on rivers and labour from Dhaka. This piece was reported over four visits between March and June 2026."
- Issue footer: next issue, "Issue 08: Char Life" (coming December 2026); subscribe link https://jol.example.com/subscribe.
- Languages: English, with the magazine name also shown in Bangla (জল).

## Constraints
- Motion: GSAP with ScrollTrigger, and Lenis for smooth scrolling (the client's developer already uses them on the magazine). Section entrances and scroll choreography only; no pinned film, no WebGL.
- Must read well on mobile at 375 px: body text at a comfortable size and measure, images and pull quotes that don't break the column, footnotes usable by thumb.
- Reduced motion: everything readable and in order, with no entrance motion and native scrolling.
- Accessible: semantic article structure, headings in order, footnotes keyboard-reachable, contrast AA.
- Stack: not specified by the client. Chosen at Step 0.

## Success
- A phone reader finishes the piece and it felt paced, like a printed feature turned by hand: sections arrive, quotes land, nothing distracts from the words.
- The motion marks structure (where a section starts, what to look at) and never delays reading.
- The issue looks like *Jol*, not like a blog template.
