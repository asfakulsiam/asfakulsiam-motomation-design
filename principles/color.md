# Color

Color is secondary to type and space. Use it with intention and restraint.

## Default stance

- Prefer near-monochrome or very limited palettes.
- One strong accent is usually enough.
- Neutrals should be slightly warm or cool rather than pure gray when possible.
- High contrast for text is non-negotiable (WCAG AA minimum, AAA preferred for body).

## Palette construction

1. Choose a dominant neutral (background and large surfaces).
2. Choose a secondary neutral for elevation or secondary surfaces.
3. Choose one accent that carries the brand or emotional temperature.
4. Optionally add one supporting accent for states (success, warning, error) if needed.

Avoid rainbow accents and multi-color gradients unless the concept specifically demands them.

## Dark mode

- Prefer true dark (near-black) over pure black when text is involved.
- Raise surface elevation with subtle lightness rather than heavy shadows.
- Keep accent colors readable; desaturate slightly if needed for comfort.

## Application rules

- Large areas of color should feel intentional, not decorative.
- Interactive elements must have clear color differentiation from static content.
- Never rely on color alone to convey meaning (pair with icon, text, or shape).

## Restraint test

If the design still works in grayscale, the color is supporting the design rather than carrying it. That is the goal.
