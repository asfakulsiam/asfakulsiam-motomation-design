The Operational Challenge

Midwest Specialty Foods sources temperature-sensitive culinary ingredients from European and South American suppliers. With perishable cargo, terminal dwell time is not merely a financial hazard: prolonged port congestion risks generator fuel exhaustion, temperature abuse, and complete inventory forfeiture.

Prior to implementing Cargoline, the operations team tracked containers across four carrier tracking websites and endless email threads with freight forwarders. Arrival dates were updated manually in NetSuite twice per week. When West Coast berth delays compounded, harbor drayage appointments were frequently scheduled either too early (paying dry-run truck charges) or too late (incurring steep marine terminal demurrage fees).

The Cargoline Solution

Midwest Foods deployed Cargoline with direct AIS vessel tracking and automated EDI 315 milestone normalization. Key implementation components included:

  • Predictive Berth Alerts: Operations managers receive automated alerts 72 hours before vessel arrival, factoring in live harbor anchor queues to schedule quayside drayage accurately.
  • Reefer Telemetry Ingestion: Continuous temperature and genset status monitoring across ocean and rail legs.
  • Two-Way NetSuite Synchronization: Verified container discharge timestamps sync automatically into NetSuite Inbound Shipment records, recalculating warehouse labor scheduling automatically.
