- **Kind:** e-commerce product
- **Archetype · mutation · signature:** catalogue · single-object · hold-to-commit

- **Type roles:** display Fraunces · text Familjen Grotesk · mono JetBrains Mono
- **What it is:** One plate from a small firing, sold like a catalogue lot: the object, its making in four steps, buyers' notes, a hold to reserve, then the rest of the shelf.

## Sections, in order

### 1. hero (`hero-shop-catalogue`)

A ceramics shop's product hero as a catalogue entry: object number, the piece held like a card you can tilt to the light, and a spec sheet with the kiln batch counting down.

- **Why it's memorable:** Each piece is numbered and specified like a museum object, because every glaze firing is different; the batch counter is the real stock.
- **Mobile:** The spec sheet drops under the object; the card doesn't tilt on touch.
- **Reduced motion:** No tilt; the counter shows the current stock.

### 2. features (`features-shop-specs`)

The making of a plate as the lot's record card: clay, throwing, glaze, firing in four ruled columns, each step with its week and its numbers. Nothing pins: on a Tier 2 product page the page budget allows 2 pinned sections, and the object's record reads best at a glance.

- **Why it's memorable:** The Catalogue's spec sheet becomes the process: each column is one line of the object's record, ruled like an auction-house lot card on grained paper.
- **Mobile:** The four columns become four ruled rows.
- **Reduced motion:** Identical; the record has no motion.

### 3. testimonial (`testimonial-shop-notes`)

Buyers' notes, pinned like the cards that come back in the post, with the plate they're about; clicking a note scatters a few small kiln stamps around it.

- **Why it's memorable:** These are the actual notes customers mail back, each tied to an object number; the stamps are the studio's real kiln marks.
- **Mobile:** Notes stack in one column; tapping scatters stamps.
- **Reduced motion:** No scatter; notes are plain.

### 4. cta (`cta-shop-hold`)

Reserving a piece from a small firing takes a deliberate press and hold, because there are nine and each one is unique; it confirms in words.

- **Why it's memorable:** The hold matches the stakes (a unique object, held for 48 hours), and the confirmation names the object number.
- **Mobile:** The hold button is full width with a large target.
- **Reduced motion:** Hold still works; the fill is linear with no flourish.

### 5. gallery (`gallery-shop-carousel`)

The rest of the firing on a curved shelf: plates and bowls stand in an arc you drag or arrow through, each with its object number and price.

- **Why it's memorable:** The Shelf archetype puts the products in a physical arrangement; the curve is the kiln shelf they came off.
- **Mobile:** The arc stays; you swipe or use the arrow buttons; square cards keep the caption inside the shelf height.
- **Reduced motion:** No arc motion; a flat, scrollable row.

### 6. pricing (`pricing-shop-glazes`)

Three glaze sets priced as catalogue lots; the set a visitor points at gets a turning border in that set's own glaze colours.

- **Why it's memorable:** The border colours are the actual glazes in each set, so the only colourful motion on the page is information about the product.
- **Mobile:** Lots stack; borders still (they turn while pressed).
- **Reduced motion:** Static glaze-coloured borders.
