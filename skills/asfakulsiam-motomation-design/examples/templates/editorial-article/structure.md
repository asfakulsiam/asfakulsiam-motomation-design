## Sections, in order

### 1. hero (`hero-editorial-broadsheet`)

A long-read's opening set as a newspaper front: masthead, dateline that decodes from the wire, a headline that comes into focus, and the first paragraphs in two columns with a drop cap.

- **Why it's memorable:** It reads as journalism before a word is read: masthead, folio and columns are the structure, and the decoding dateline marks the piece as filed, not published by a template.
- **Mobile:** Columns collapse to one; the masthead rules stay; the headline wraps at a smaller size.
- **Reduced motion:** The dateline and headline appear complete and sharp.

### 2. features (`feature-editorial-pullquote`)

The middle of a long read: a pull quote that inks in as it's read, between body columns, with a thin reading rail showing how far through the article you are.

- **Why it's memorable:** Broadsheet devices doing their real jobs: the pull quote slows the reader at the article's turn, and the rail answers "how long is left" without a timer.
- **Mobile:** One column; the quote is full width at a smaller size; the rail stays at the top.
- **Reduced motion:** The quote is fully inked; the rail still tracks position (it moves only when the reader scrolls).

### 3. footer (`footer-editorial-folio`)

The paper's footer is a folio line plus "recently read": the stories this reader has opened before (kept on their device), with the sections and the masthead again.

- **Why it's memorable:** Recently-read is genuinely useful for a long-read magazine and costs nothing (local storage, no tracking); the folio closes the print metaphor.
- **Mobile:** Columns stack; the list is full width.
- **Reduced motion:** Nothing animates; the list is static.
